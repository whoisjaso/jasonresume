# Outreach desk

How Jason Obawemimo shows up on LinkedIn, Reddit, Facebook groups, X and YouTube without sounding like a bot, a brochure, or an AI. This file is the brief for the weekly content routine and for anyone drafting on Jason's behalf. Every draft goes to Jason for approval before it is posted. Nothing is auto-posted.

## What we are trying to make true

When a hiring manager, a recruiter or a dealer searches "Jason Obawemimo" before a call, they find a person who owns and runs a real business (Triple J Auto Investment in Houston), built the systems it runs on, and says exactly what each one does and does not do. Obavia comes after that, described as what it is: a dealership sale desk in development. Authority comes from specificity and restraint, not volume.

## Who we are talking to

First, people hiring for AI implementation, workflow automation and CRM work: hiring managers, recruiters, and operators who want someone who has shipped those systems inside a working business. The pain we write about: leads that land in three places and get followed up in none, inbound calls that never reach the CRM, records nobody trusts, and a process that lives in one person's head.

Second, and smaller, Texas independent dealers, for Obavia. The pain: a sale that stalls on the paperwork, a title packet that comes back, the 130-U, the tax math.

## The voice

Pulled from Jason's own recording, his writing, and the copy on obavia.co and thetriplejauto.com.

- Second person. Set up the reader's situation before the point. "If your leads come in from three places and nobody owns the follow-up, this is for you."
- Plain words. Contractions. "Gonna" and "honestly" are fine in speech and in casual platforms, not in LinkedIn long form.
- Blunt when it counts. On Reddit: "Nobody trusts your CRM." On LinkedIn, the same thought reads "A CRM the staff doesn't trust is a list with a login."
- "We" for the businesses. "I" for what Jason did or saw.
- Specific nouns: lead, booked appointment, deal, title packet, the 130-U, CRM record, webhook, follow-up, SOP, the buyer's exact words. Never "solutions", "leverage", "seamless", "streamline", "elevate", "game-changer", "unlock".
- No tidy groups of three. No "it's not X, it's Y". No em dashes. No emoji in body text. No hashtags on LinkedIn beyond two at the very end, none on Reddit.
- Vary sentence length. Let one sentence be four words.

## What Jason runs and built

Jason owns Triple J Auto Investment and Rental Operations in Houston and has run it as Owner and Operations Lead since August 2024: pricing, inventory, vendors, logistics, financing and title processing. Since June 2025 he has personally closed 53 vehicle sales and collected $206,777 in sale proceeds.

He built the three systems it runs on:

- The custom dealership CRM, whose sale desk is Handle a Sale. One system. TypeScript, React, Next.js, PostgreSQL, Supabase and Vercel, with Edge Functions for the voice and texting webhooks. Three staff use it daily across inventory, deals and follow-up. The sale desk asks one plain question at a time, scans the buyer's license once so every form fills itself, does the registration math, routes the deal to the right documents, works in English or Spanish, and the customer signs on their phone or in person.
- Inbound voice AI (Retell AI, Bland AI, Twilio) that books appointments into the CRM and live-transfers unresolved calls to staff.
- A prospecting workflow (Python, the Claude API, n8n) that researches Facebook Marketplace leads, writes findings to the CRM and generates SOP-based reply text for automatic follow-up. It is Triple J's live lead-research workflow and is never Obavia's Reach.

## What Obavia is

Obavia (obavia.co) is a dealership sale desk in development for Texas independent dealers. "Every sale, start to signed." Jason founded it in September 2024, and it grew out of Handle a Sale, the sale desk in the CRM he built for Triple J. One question per screen; cash, buy here pay here and bank financing; Texas sales tax, title, registration and the dealer's doc fee worked out; the bill of sale and the 130-U filled from the deal. Text it to sign, Reach and dealer websites are in development, not live. No price is published. Early access is a conversation, not an account.

## The claims boundary (never cross it)

Obavia is in development. Never say it is live, that dealers use it, that it has customers, or that it produces results. Never state a conversion rate, a close rate, a percentage, or an outcome as fact. Never quote a price; none is published. Never borrow a case study or a testimonial. People and figures in Obavia's demos and films are fictional, and any post that shows them says so.

The Triple J figures are quoted exactly as written: 53 vehicle sales and $206,777 in sale proceeds since June 2025, and for the voice AI, 41 inbound calls, 14 booked appointments and 4 CRM-matched vehicle sales per month across August and September 2026 ($15,000 a month in sale proceeds). Keep the word "CRM-matched". Never say the voice AI caused those sales, and never turn any of these numbers into a rate or a percentage. Never post a Triple J customer's details.

If a comment asks "does Obavia work," the answer is "it is not live yet. I will not put a number on it until there is one to publish." If a comment asks about Triple J's sale desk, describe what it does (one plain question at a time, one license scan that fills every form, the right documents with nothing missing and nothing extra, English or Spanish) and nothing it has not done.

## What performs, per platform, and how we use it

**LinkedIn.** What travels: a first line that names a specific situation, short paragraphs with white space, one observation from operating a real business, a soft close with no link in the body (link in the first comment). Build notes from the lot (the data model behind a deal, wiring inbound calls into the CRM, the SOP behind a follow-up reply) outperform announcements. Format: 120 to 220 words, first line under 12 words, ends on a question or a plain statement, never "thoughts?".

**Reddit** (r/smallbusiness, r/Entrepreneur, r/automation, r/n8n). What travels: a genuine question or a story from doing the work, written by someone who obviously does it, zero links, zero product mentions in the post. Obavia surfaces only if someone asks, and then as one sentence with the claims boundary. Read each sub's rules before drafting. Most ban self-promotion outright; a post that looks like marketing gets removed and damages the account. Titles are plain and specific: "Inbound calls come in after hours. How do you get them into the CRM?"

**Facebook groups** (Texas independent dealer groups, small-business owner groups). What travels: a short story from a real week on the lot, a question to the group. Same rule as Reddit: give first, no links, Obavia only when asked. There is no API for posting into groups; drafts are copy and paste.

**X.** What travels: one observation per post, under 200 characters, no thread unless the story needs it. Voice can be the Reddit register.

**YouTube and Shorts.** What travels: a weekly long on building one piece of the system (the CRM data model, the inbound call flow, the lead-research workflow), cut into three shorts. The Handle a Sale film and the Obavia desk film are the reference for tone; every person and figure in a demo is fictional and the caption says so.
