Rewrite the note.

Fictional call, fictional buyer.

A setter left this for the closer:

“Good fit. Hot lead. Call him.”

Tap each piece of the call. It drops into the line where a closer needs it.

  1. The buyer said
  2. This call is for
  3. Who decides
  4. What we promised or ruled out

Now the closer's first line writes itself:

“You said you never knew who was on your account. Before your partner sees anything, let me show you how the handoff would work.”

That's the job: carry the buyer's words from one seat to the next.

The two roles.

Appointment setter

Phone, text and DMs

You reach agency owners who have shown interest, hold a real conversation about their sales floor, and book them onto a thirty-minute call. You're not reading a script. You're asking where their last deal leaked, and listening to the exact words they use.

  • The pay structure is in writing before you do any work.
  • You've set appointments or sold by phone before and can say what happened after.
  • You'll say plainly that Obavia is in development. Nobody gets booked on a promise.

Closer

Runs the thirty-minute call

You run the call with me on it at first, then alone. An owner walks you through how a lead gets from the first call to cash on their floor, you listen for the words they choose, and together you decide whether Obavia fits. You have to be able to say "this is not for you" on the call.

  • The pay structure is in writing before you do any work.
  • You've closed high-ticket offers by phone or video, ideally with agency owners.
  • You'll never claim Obavia is live, has customers, or produces results. If that bothers you, this is the wrong room.
